AIC — Learned the Hard Way

Field notes from Intrinsic's AI for Industry Challenge — sim-to-real cable insertion, 2026.

Three workspaces, six weeks, and a deadline that slid into the next quarter taught me more about the gap between "the policy should work" and "the policy actually inserts the plug" than any paper did. What follows is the short list of mistakes I do not intend to repeat.

1. Don't fake spawn poses with motion controllers.

Five attempts to reposition the arm near the socket via VF pre-rolls, OSC waypoints, two-phase OSC, and an IK + spawn-cone teleport all failed — overshoot, below-table crashes, "two motions" artifacts, in-table spawns. The right answer is the boring one: drive the arm to a good pose with teleop, read the joints off the property panel, and hardcode them into init_state.joint_pos.

2. Open-loop control loses to flexible-cable dynamics.

An 80-link flexible cable under an impedance controller produces sag, swing, and lateral levering. Pure P-control oscillates around the swing-induced steady-state error; only the integral term reliably zeroes it. Vector-field control is safe only for monotonic descent — never feed it an above-port waypoint, it will overshoot upward.

3. Read-after-write returns stale physics state.

robot.data.body_pos_w right after write_root_pose_to_sim in Isaac, or a TF lookup right after a Gazebo respawn, both return pre-write data. Always step the sim once or gate on a fresh /clock tick before consuming pose data. Many "sign error" and "crashed into the table" bugs traced back to this.

4. Sim-to-sim is not free.

Isaac Lab and Gazebo do not agree: gravity-comp torques differ by 2×, the IsaacLab insertion axis is ~35° off because card rotation is not applied to a hardcoded offset, and SC-port collisions are unreliable in both Gazebo and MuJoCo. Effort and torque features are non-portable — train on position / velocity if you want the policy to transfer.

5. Filter at collection time, not at training time.

Bad trajectories left on disk get re-read every epoch and silently leak in when filter logic drifts. Classify and drop per-trial outcomes the moment they happen — or write them to a quarantine directory so they cannot be picked up by the loader.

6. Long unattended runs die at the same threshold.

CablePlugin segfaults around 140–150 sequential spawn/delete cycles — an upstream entity leak in detachable joints. Batch collection in chunks of fewer than 120 episodes and tear Gazebo down and back up between batches.

7. Controller state carries between episodes.

Without an explicit command-buffer zero and a joint-velocity ~0 check, the first step of every new trial gets a "kick" from a stale commanded pose. Matches the upstream init-sag report and corrupts the first frames of the bag.

8. Full robot+cable respawn breaks the impedance controller.

Sudden cable load at spawn-time sags the arm, and a stiffness ramp introduces new pathologies (weird transients, XY divergence). Board-only respawn — delete and re-add just the task board — sidesteps the whole problem.

9. Don't trust the Gazebo touch sensor alone.

The contact collision box is 0.25 mm thin and the plug routinely misses it. Add a proximity-based fallback (e.g. 3 mm Z threshold) before declaring insertion done.

10. F/T tare does not tare the gravity bias.

The wrist Fz reads ~20 N at init from the gripper's own weight, and contact actually decreases Fz as the normal force partially cancels gravity. Threshold on the delta from steady-state, not the absolute value.

Underlying principle.

Every time I reached for a clever solution (controller-driven spawn, stiffness ramps, multi-phase motion plans) it cost me a session and produced nothing. The dumb fix — change a constant, hardcode an init, restart the sim — was almost always within reach and almost always shipped.

Source notes live across three local workspaces (ws_aic, ws_aic_mine, ws_aic_second) and the IsaacLab/aic fork; this page is the distilled public version. Last updated 2026-06-13.
